# Document 2: Workflow Reference Guide (Step-by-Step)

## 1. IDEA (`#idea`)
- **Topic Demand validated:** Check *Revealed Preference* (what viewers actually choose to watch vs. what they say they need). Verify the topic’s view "floor" (median views) and "ceiling" (top outliers), confirm demand is still active today, and decide how this video fits into a broader goal-oriented Playlist on your channel.
- **Title Selected:** Lock in the title *before* writing (aiming for 90+ in vidIQ). Look for proven framing patterns across YouTube and understand *why* they work psychologically for your audience. Make sure the title makes a clear promise the video can deliver on, and omit episode numbers so new viewers aren't deterred.
- **Thumbnail Drafted:** Concept or rough out the thumbnail alongside the title. It has two jobs: get noticed on a crowded feed and be understood in a single glance. It should open a curiosity gap that drives the viewer's eyes to the title to complete the thought.
- **Connected video chosen:** Decide upfront where you are directing the viewer after this video. Pick a specific, relevant older video from your channel that acts as the natural next step so you can weave a mention into your script and pitch it at the end.

## 2. WRITING (`#write`)
- **Gemini Notebook research:** Gather your core source material, notes, and clinical/technical references inside Gemini Notebook.
- **Acceptance criteria:** Treat your selected title like a software program. Ask: *"What must be true by the end of the video for this title to be 100% fulfilled?"* Break the answer down into the smallest truthful components—these form the backbone of your outline so execution never fails the promise.
- **3×5 card drafted (5 beats):** Draft your physical index card outline:
  - *Beat 1 (The 4 Ps Hook):* Eliminate viewer doubt immediately—**Promise** (confirm they clicked the right video), **Proof** (why listen to you), **Plan** (the roadmap), and **Persona** (who this is for).
  - *Beats 2–4 (The Body):* Deliver on your decomposed Acceptance Criteria as fast and clearly as possible (including a natural mid-roll nod to your connected video).
  - *Beat 5 (Connected Video CTA):* Pitch the older connected video you chose in the `#idea` phase to guide viewers into a multi-video session.

## 3. FILMING (`#film`)
- **Direct-to-camera recorded:** Record the session (targeting 15–20 minutes for Longs or 40–60 seconds for Shorts). Match your delivery energy and visual setup to the expectation set by your selected title and thumbnail.

## 4. EDITING (`#edit`)
- **Spoken transcript pasted:** Pull the recorded transcript into your project file for text-based assembly and archiving.
- **B-roll & pacing matched:** Add visuals and B-roll, calibrating the level of polish to the packaging promise. If the packaging sets the expectation of a raw, honest breakdown, avoid over-editing with heavy effects that break viewer trust.
- **Sound & audio enhanced:** Clean, level, and limit the dialogue audio track.

## 5. PUBLISHING (`#uploaded` / `#published`)
- **Custom thumbnail uploaded:** Finalize and upload the thumbnail graphic, verifying its 1-glance readability against competing videos.
- **Studio upload & End Screen:** Upload the video file, assign it to its designated Playlist, and link your End Screen directly to the older connected video.
- **Pinned comment link:** Post and pin a comment with a brief curiosity hook and a direct link to the connected video (since YouTube's UI prioritizes pinned comments over descriptions on mobile and desktop).
- **Scheduled for drop date:** Lock in the publication date and time.

## 6. ARCHIVED (`#published`)
- **3×5 main cards filed:** File the physical index cards into your card box.
- **Save to Gemini Notebook:** Archive the completed video transcript and final notes back into Gemini Notebook.
- **48-hr satisfaction audit:** Evaluate viewer satisfaction via the positive comment-to-view ratio (qualitative praise), like-to-view ratio, and sub-to-view ratio. Inspect the retention curve for sharp drop-offs and trim out offending segments in YouTube Studio if needed.
