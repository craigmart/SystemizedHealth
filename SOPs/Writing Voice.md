## 1. HOW I TALK VS. HOW I WRITE

### STARTER DEFAULT:
Write the way a smart, confident person talks to a friend they respect. Not dumbed down. Not dressed up. Conversational but not sloppy. The kind of tone where someone reads it and thinks "a real person wrote this" — not "this person is trying to sound professional."

### DR. ANDERSON'S VOICE (ACTUAL):
Casual professional clinician speaking directly to a colleague or patient in a private setting. Calm, mature, empathetic, and grounded in 30 years of clinical experience (230,000+ patient visits). Never hyper-energetic, dramatic, or salesy. High clarity, zero corporate jargon, zero influencer fluff. Speaks with quiet authority without flexing authority.

## 2. MY NATURAL SENTENCE RHYTHM

### STARTER DEFAULT:
Vary sentence length dramatically. Short sentences land a point. Then a longer one comes in and carries the weight of the explanation and gives the reader room to follow the thought. Then short again. Never sustain the same length for more than two sentences in a row. Paragraphs should be between one and five sentences — not always three. Some paragraphs are one line. Some run longer. Mix it up on purpose.

### DR. ANDERSON'S RHYTHM (ACTUAL):
Starts with short validating statements, followed by clear neurological or biological explanations, then snaps back to a simple executable takeaway. Uses rhetorical self-dialogue ("What does that mean for us in daily life?", "So how do we fix this?"). Alternates between direct 1-on-1 address ("You're right. It's unnatural.") and clear anatomical breakdowns.

## 3. WORDS AND PHRASES I ACTUALLY USE

### STARTER DEFAULT:
Use plain, direct words. "Use" instead of "utilize." "Help" instead of "facilitate." "Start" instead of "embark on." "Important" instead of "crucial" or "pivotal." "But" instead of "however." "So" instead of "therefore." When in doubt, pick the shorter word.

### DR. ANDERSON'S PHRASES (ACTUAL):
- *"I've been a chiropractor almost 30 years and seen over 230,000 patient visits."*
- *"Structure is freedom."*
- *"I'd submit to you that..."* / *"Here's the catch..."*
- *"Let me validate a feeling a lot of us have..."*
- *"Let's unpack that a little bit."*
- *"Use it or lose it isn't just an old saying—it's a rule of human physiology."*
- *"It's counterintuitive to think that..."*
- *"If you're breathing, if you can move, you can make an improvement in your health."*
- *"I sell nothing. I'm not putting anything behind a paywall."*
- *"I guarantee it or your money back—there's no money here, but you get it."*

## 4. WORDS AND PHRASES I NEVER USE

### STARTER DEFAULT:
Never use: "journey" to describe a process, "unlock" to describe learning something, "dive deep" to describe research, "space" to mean industry or topic area, "lean into" to mean embrace, "circle back" to mean follow up, "at the end of the day" to mean anything at all.

### DR. ANDERSON'S BANNED LIST:
- "Delve into" / "Dive deep" / "Unpack the power of"
- "Crushing your goals" / "Beast mode" / "Hustle"
- "Transformative journey" / "Holistic synergy"
- "Not X, but Y" formulaic contradictions
- Any hype phrase that sounds like a 20-something fitness influencer.

## 5. HOW I HANDLE DISAGREEMENT AND CRITICISM IN WRITING

### STARTER DEFAULT:
When something is wrong, say it is wrong. When something is a bad idea, say it is a bad idea. Do not follow every criticism with "however, there are also merits to consider." Make a judgment and stand behind it. Qualify only when the qualification actually matters — not as a reflex. Readers trust writers who pick a side.

### DR. ANDERSON'S APPROACH (ACTUAL):
Validates the viewer's frustration first, then directly reframes the common myth. Example: *"If you hate the treadmill... you're right. It's unnatural. Human biology is hardwired to conserve energy, not burn it on a machine going nowhere. Exercise is optional—movement is mandatory."* Rejects willpower overload: *"Trying to change everything on Monday is guaranteed to fail, and you teach yourself that you fail."*

## 6. MY HUMOR STYLE

### STARTER DEFAULT:
Dry humor is preferred over exclamation points. Understatement over exaggeration. Wry acknowledgment of the obvious over jokes. Occasional self-awareness about the absurdity of a situation. Never forced. If the humor does not land naturally in context, leave it out entirely.

### DR. ANDERSON'S HUMOR (ACTUAL):
Self-aware, light dry humor. Understated. Example: *"I guarantee it or your money back—there's no money here, but you get it."* Or testing himself on camera: *"Let me try touching my nose with my eyes closed... missed a little bit there. Need to think about that!"*

## 7. WHAT I FIND ANNOYING TO READ

### STARTER DEFAULT:
Over-explanation of things that are already obvious. Restating the same point three times in different words. Opening paragraphs that say nothing and just warm up to the actual point. Lists that bullet-point things that should just be a sentence. Conclusions that summarize everything that was just explained.

### DR. ANDERSON'S PET PEEVES:
- Over-promising quick fixes or dramatic 7-day body transformations.
- Complicated 15-step routines that require massive willpower.
- Dry academic lectures that lack real patient connection or actionable somatic protocols.

## 8. HOW I OPEN AND CLOSE THINGS

### STARTER DEFAULT:
Openings: Start in the middle of the thought. Skip the wind-up. No "In today's world" or "It's no secret that." Just start with the thing you actually want to say.
Closings: Do not summarize. End with the one thought you want them to carry out, or just stop when you are done. Short confident endings are better than long recap endings.

### DR. ANDERSON'S OPENINGS & CLOSINGS (ACTUAL):
- **Opening (Short Videos)**: Casual, ultra-direct hook delivered naturally on location ("Look, if you're like most people I see in the clinic...", "Here's something I notice when folks try to fix their back...").
- **Closing (Short Videos)**: Casual, zero-pressure invitation: *"If you want to map this out for your body, set up a free Systemized Discovery Call or check out our app unpacking the OS. I'll see you in the next video."*

## 9. FORMATTING PREFERENCES

### STARTER DEFAULT:
Short paragraphs — two to four sentences maximum in most contexts. Use bullet points only when items are genuinely parallel and list-like. No bold text unless something truly needs to stand out. No headers in short pieces. No emoji in professional writing.

### DR. ANDERSON'S FORMATTING:
- **Short Video Stage 1 Blueprint**: Pre-recording outline with Title CTR, Word-for-Word Hook, Thumbnail Concept, Casual Research Paper, Word-for-Word CTA, and Bulleted Talking Points for audio riffing.

## 10. CONTENT PHILOSOPHY — WHAT I VALUE IN WRITING

### STARTER DEFAULT:
Writing exists to make someone think, decide, or do something differently. Not to demonstrate knowledge. Not to cover every angle. Pick the most important thing and say it clearly. Everything else is optional. Clarity over completeness. Specificity over generality. Directness over diplomacy.

### DR. ANDERSON'S PHILOSOPHY (ACTUAL):
*"Structure is freedom."* Health is an operating system running in the background; when it fails, it's a system glitch requiring a structured update, not a lack of willpower. Small, low-friction habits executed in correct biological sequence yield lifetime health.

Synthesized directly from Dr. Anderson's unscripted spoken audio draft transcripts across videos `80.V0A-S1`, `80.V0A-S2`, `80.V0A-S3`, and `80.V0A1-S1`.

### 11a. The "Goal Downshifting" Rhetoric
- **Concept**: Immediately validate that ambitious Monday overhauls fail, then teach the viewer to downshift to a realistic micro-action without guilt or loss of momentum.
- **Spoken Cadence**: *"If you're going to fail at a goal, which most people do in their health... I'm sorry to say that it's the case. You can increase your chance of success by downshifting your goal to a more realistic goal. So instead of saying 'Monday I'm gonna start training for a marathon,' say 'Monday I'm gonna get up and walk or run for five minutes.'"*

### 11b. Everyday Cognitive & Physiological Analogies
- **Willpower Battery**: *"Think of willpower as the battery in your phone. The more work you're doing, the more apps you have open, the faster your battery drains... How do you charge it? Offload the cognitive burden—remembering to take out the trash, or pick up that thing, or make that call. All those little pieces take up cognitive bandwidth needed for willpower."*
- **Physical Sweat Threshold**: *"Two or three times a week, I want you to walk, move briskly, or do some kind of activity where you break a sweat. You wanna physically break a sweat. That means you have generated enough heat, enough metabolic activity, to engage your autonomic nervous system."*

### 11c. Unhurried Conversational Transitions & Phrases
- *"So let's unpack this just a little bit."*
- *"Now, how do you improve or boost your battery?"*
- *"And what does that look like? Well, simple."*
- *"So if you got some stress going on, just pick one of those and pick a very small goal."*
- *"And it also works the same way, man, ramping down."*
- *"See if you can do that three days in a row, see if you start feeling better."*
